# Store Listing Draft

## Subtitle

Ocean gas capacity

## Promotional Text

See how warming changes seawater's capacity to hold dissolved gases, starting with a cited CFC solubility proxy and NOAA monthly data.

## Description

Haline shows how ocean warming changes the sea's capacity to hold dissolved gases.

The first release starts with a cited CFC-11 and CFC-12 solubility proxy. It combines NOAA monthly global ocean temperature anomalies with Warner & Weiss solubility coefficients, then reports the modeled thermal solubility shift against a fixed 15°C, salinity-35 baseline.

The goal is restrained climate instrumentation: one carefully sourced calculation at a time, with the method visible beside the number.

What Haline includes:

- NOAA global ocean monthly anomaly status
- CFC solubility shift percent for the latest source month
- A 120-month history chart with zero-anchored warming colors
- NOAA 1901-2000 base-period context
- A method summary with source links and citations
- A public roadmap for future dissolved-gas modules, starting with oxygen

Haline is designed for people who want ocean climate signals without overstated claims. The CFC figure is a modeled thermal solubility proxy. It is not a direct ocean gas uptake result, not a flux estimate, and not an emissions inversion.

The roadmap expands from the CFC proxy toward dissolved oxygen saturation, custom baselines, export tools, and local coastal context, while keeping the same rule: every new figure must be sourced, tested, and explained before it appears in the app.

Sources include NOAA Climate at a Glance global ocean data, Warner & Weiss (1985), and Brandon W. Caris's Zenodo preprint collection, The Coupled Marine Engine Framework, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20804280.
